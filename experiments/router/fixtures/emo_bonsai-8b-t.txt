PROMPT:
My best friend just got the promotion I was passed over for, and she wants me to celebrate with her tonight. I'm genuinely happy for her and I also kind of hate her right now, and I hate that I feel that way. What do I do?

--- ANSWER ---
It's completely normal to feel a mix of emotions in this situation—pride for your friend, disappointment for yourself, and even a bit of resentment or frustration. You're not alone in feeling this way, and it's okay to acknowledge those feelings without letting them define your relationship or your actions.

Here are some steps you can take to navigate this:

---

### 1. **Acknowledge Your Feelings**
It's important to recognize that you're feeling this way. You don't have to "fix" it right away. It's okay to feel sad, frustrated, or even a little resentful. You're allowed to process these emotions.

---

### 2. **Reflect on the Situation**
Ask yourself:
- Was the promotion based on merit, or was it a personal decision?
- Did you have any input or opportunity to be considered?
- Are there any patterns in your relationship or how you've been treated in the past?

This reflection can help you understand your feelings better and whether they're rooted in something specific or more general.

---

### 3. **Consider the Context of the Celebration**
Your friend wants to celebrate with you, and she's genuinely happy. You can choose to:
- **Celebrate with her**—even if you're not in the same place emotionally. You can show up, have a good time, and let the moment be.
- **Celebrate on your own** if you're not ready to be around her. It's okay to take a break or do something that makes you feel better.

---

### 4. **Communicate (If You're Ready)**
If you feel comfortable, you could say something like:
> "I'm really happy for you, and I'm glad you're celebrating with me. I just feel a bit disappointed that I wasn't considered, and I'm still working through that. But I'm here for the celebration, and I hope we can talk about it later."

This gives her the space to respond if she wants, and it shows that you're not just being negative—there's a deeper part of you that wants to be understood.

---

### 5. **Give Yourself Time to Adjust**
It's not uncommon to feel this way after being passed over. You don't have to be "okay" right away. Give yourself time to process and let the emotions settle. You can still be happy for your friend and still feel the way you do.

---

### 6. **Focus on What You Can Control**
You can't change the outcome of the promotion, but you can:
- **Celebrate your friend's success** in a meaningful way.
- **Work on your own goals** and growth.
- **Reconnect with your own values** and what you want from your career and relationships.

---

### 7. **If the Resentment Is Strong, Consider Distance**
If the feeling of resentment is overwhelming and you're not sure how to address it, it might be helpful to take a step back. You don't have to be in the same place emotionally, and sometimes distance can help you gain perspective.

---

### Final Thought:
You're allowed to feel this way. It doesn't mean you're ungrateful or unkind. It just means you're human. And your friend is still your friend—she's celebrating, and you can still be part of that celebration, even if it's not the way you expected.

If you're still struggling, talking to a trusted friend, mentor, or therapist can be really helpful. You don't have to go through this alone.
