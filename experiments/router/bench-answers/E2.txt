# E2 | class emotional | final T1 | escalations 0 | flagged False
--- QUERY ---
My best friend got the promotion I was passed over for and wants to celebrate tonight. I'm happy for her and I kind of hate her and I hate that I feel that way. What do I do?
--- ANSWER ---
It's completely normal to feel a mix of emotions in this situation—pride for your friend, sadness or frustration about not getting the promotion, and even a bit of resentment. These feelings are valid, and it's okay to feel them. But here's what you can do to move forward in a healthy way:

---

### 1. **Acknowledge Your Feelings**
It's important to recognize that you're not alone in feeling this way. You're allowed to feel upset, even if it's not the most constructive emotion. Try not to suppress them—let yourself feel them, but also understand that they don't define your relationship with your friend.

---

### 2. **Reflect on the Situation**
Ask yourself:
- Was the promotion based on merit, or was there something else?
- Did you do everything you could to be considered?
- Is this a one-time thing, or is there a pattern?

This reflection can help you understand your own feelings and whether there's something you can learn or improve from the experience.

---

### 3. **Talk to Your Friend (If You're Comfortable)**
If you feel like it, you could say something like:
> "I'm really happy for you, and I know you've worked hard for this. I just feel a little sad that I wasn't considered. I don't want to be negative, but I'm still processing it."

This opens the door for her to understand your perspective without making you feel like you're being confrontational. It also gives you a chance to express your feelings in a way that's honest and not hurtful.

---

### 4. **Focus on What You Can Control**
Instead of dwelling on the promotion, think about:
- What you're working on now
- How you can grow from this experience
- What you can do to improve your chances in the future

This helps shift your focus from what you lost to what you can gain.

---

### 5. **Celebrate Her Success, Even If It's Bittersweet**
You can still enjoy the celebration. Maybe you can say something like:
> "I'm so proud of you, and I know you're going to do great. I just wish I had been considered too."

This shows that you're happy for her, but also that you're not okay with the outcome.

---

### 6. **Give Yourself Time to Process**
You don't have to feel this way all the time. It's okay to take a moment to process your emotions. You can even write them down in a journal or talk to someone you trust.

---

### 7. **Consider the Relationship**
If this is a recurring issue or if you feel like the resentment is growing, it might be worth having a deeper conversation about how you feel. But if you're not ready for that, it's okay to just let the situation be for now.

---

### 8. **Don’t Let It Define You**
This one promotion doesn’t define your worth or your future. You're still capable, still valuable, and still deserving of success. Use this as motivation to work harder, not as a reason to feel defeated.

---

### Final Thought:
You're allowed to feel this way, and it's okay to be conflicted. But don't let the resentment take over your friendship or your self-esteem. You can still be supportive, happy for your friend, and work on your own goals.

If you're still struggling, consider talking to a counselor or therapist—they can help you process these complex emotions in a safe space.

You're not alone in this.
