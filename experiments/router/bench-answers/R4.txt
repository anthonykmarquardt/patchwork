--- QUERY ---
A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost? Explain why the intuitive answer is wrong.
--- ANSWER ---
Let's break this down step by step.

### Problem:
A bat and a ball cost **$1.10 in total**.  
The bat costs **$1.00 more than the ball**.  
How much does the ball cost?

---

### Step 1: Define variables
Let the cost of the ball be **$x**.

Then the cost of the bat is **$x + $1.00**.

---

### Step 2: Set up the equation
The total cost of the bat and the ball is:
$$
x + (x + 1.00) = 1.10
$$

Simplify:
$$
2x + 1.00 = 1.10
$$

Subtract 1.00 from both sides:
$$
2x = 0.10
$$

Divide both sides by 2:
$$
x = 0.05
$$

So, the ball costs **$0.05**.

---

### Step 3: Intuitive answer explanation
Some people might think the ball costs **$0.10**, because they might assume the bat is $1.00 more than the ball, and then add them together. But that's **not correct**.

Let’s test that:

- If the ball costs **$0.10**, then the bat would cost **$1.10**.
- Total cost would be **$0.10 + $1.10 = $1.20**, which is **more than $1.10**.

So, the **intuitive answer** of $0.10 is **incorrect** because it doesn't match the total cost of $1.10.

---

### Final Answer:
The ball costs **$0.05**.

---

### Why the intuitive answer is wrong:
The intuitive answer assumes that the bat is $1.00 more than the ball, and then adds them together. However, this leads to a **total cost that exceeds the given total of $1.10**, which is not possible. Therefore, the ball must cost **$0.05**, and the bat must cost **$1.05** to make the total cost **$1.10**.
